A learning simulation—not a legal determination. Immediate safety, assistance and enforcement duties are never negotiable.

Cooperative classroom game · 25–30 minutes

Facilitator guide

How to run Break the Chain

Before the activity

  1. Print the game pack on A4 paper or card and cut out the 24 instrument cards.
  2. Shuffle all cards together. With eight participants, deal exactly three to each person.
  3. Project this game and keep control of the facilitator panel.
  4. Explain that card categories represent different contributions—not equal legal responsibility.

For each link

  1. Read the risk aloud and start the 2 minute 30 second timer.
  2. Participants describe relevant cards without showing them and agree a joint response.
  3. Select Reveal response check. Tick the three outcomes their response covers.
  4. Break the link when all three outcomes are covered. Otherwise leave it unresolved and continue.

Facilitation principles

  • There is no single correct card combination. Reward defensible alternatives.
  • Do not delay urgent protection while dialogue takes place.
  • Worker voice supports detection and sustainable solutions; workers are not responsible for violations against them.
  • ILO indicators are clues for inquiry, not a numerical test or automatic legal finding.

Debrief together

  1. Which information was easiest—or hardest—to surface?
  2. Which actions had to happen immediately?
  3. What remedied harm, and what prevented recurrence?
  4. What would have been missing if one constituency were absent?
  5. Who held legal power, and who held knowledge or worker trust?

Social dialogue strengthens prevention and implementation. It does not replace protection, remedy or enforcement.
